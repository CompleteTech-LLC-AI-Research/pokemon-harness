## Why this took six rounds

packages from a tree other than the checkout under test. Every round of

traceback, or worse, as a PASS.

## The shape of the final fix

- `_resolve_path` is the only place the module coerces untrusted data to a
  `Path`, and it converts **inside** the guard.
- The clause is `except BaseException`, not an enumeration. The input is
  interpreter and install-metadata output, so "this path is unusable" must
  hold for *every* way it can fail to be one. Narrowing it to a tuple
  reintroduces the escape the moment a caller passes something new.
- `KeyboardInterrupt` / `SystemExit` are re-raised at every site. Pinned by
  direct rows, because the namespace rows reach an interrupt through
  `_is_within` first and so cannot distinguish the two re-raise sites.
- Messages go through `_describe`, never a bare f-string: the value being
  reported can itself have a hostile `__str__`.
- The empty-origin refusal is scoped to `str`/`bytes` on purpose. A path-like
  with `__len__` returning 0 is falsy but usable, and a bare
  `if not candidate` would refuse a real path. That over-broad form was
  reproduced *during* verification, not after merge, and is pinned by its
  own row against a distinct killing test.
